# Area 10 — Money psychology and cost-splitting (light scan)

**Decision:**
- **v0 handles no money.** It shows the cost per moment and learns *feeling per £* (D10). For the weekend test, participants pay venues directly, which respects the legal gates.
- **Later:** pay-by-bank through a licensed payment initiation provider (TrueLayer, Yapily or GoCardless) as their customer, never holding funds, plus a simple split ledger.
- **Cost: 0 hours now.** Payments later: about 10 hours plus compliance review.

## What the evidence says

- **Experiences beat possessions**, partly because they build relationships. In two surveys and two experiments, experiential purchases made people happier than material ones (Van Boven & Gilovich 2003). That supports tagging *social pull* and group trips.
- **Paying ahead makes consumption feel free.** Under "prospective accounting", consumption that is already paid for is enjoyed as if it were free, and paying before consumption hurts less than paying after (Prelec & Loewenstein 1998). This supports prepaying and auto-splitting, once the legal gates allow it.
- **Payment is a commitment device for turning up.** In vendor data on 860+ events, median no-show was 28% for free events vs 17% for paid ones. In one experiment a $100 fee cut registrations from 84 to 55 but raised attendance from 38 to 50 (industry sources, search-verified only). This is directly relevant to the weekend test's "turn up" criterion.

## Gaps / risks

- **Until the legal gates clear, commitment must be non-monetary**, for example a confirmation 24 hours before, a named buddy, or a small group chat. Measure no-shows in the weekend test.
- **Money handling is inside the FCA perimeter.** Stay a customer of a licensed provider, with no stored funds and no credit (`CLAUDE.md` guardrail).
- **Refunds and disputes for stranger trips** are a support cost for the Phase 2 model.
