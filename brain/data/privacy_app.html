Privacy notice: ODYS (test app)

Last updated: 8 October 2026. This covers the ODYS test app (called "Days out" until 2 October 2026), which is in testing through Apple's TestFlight.

Who is responsible

Abram, an individual in London, is testing an idea for a travel planning app. This is research: nothing is sold. Contact: abram@abdurakhmonbek.com.

What we collect

  • Your answers in the app: the best hour of a recent day out, why you go out, your reactions to 10 London places (and whether you had no preference or weren't sure), your 10 "this or that" choices, and the optional "about you" questions (budget, who you usually go with, morning or night, how active, what interests you) and photo questions. Your budget and interests shape your plans now; the others help us test the idea. We no longer ask what foods you avoid or where you grew up, because the plans didn't use them.
  • The day plans the app makes for you, and the 1–5 ratings you give each stop.
  • A few app events, such as finishing the questions or asking for another plan, with their time.
  • Anything you write in Help & safety, and the replies to it. If you leave a phone number or email there, it's used only to reply to that message, and deleted as soon as it's sorted, or after 30 days at the latest.
  • Where you're based (London, elsewhere in the UK, or visiting from abroad) and, if you give it, your town or city. We use it to choose which questions to ask and to plan your days, and to count visitors separately in our results. We never ask your nationality, and never use where you're from to match or filter people.
  • If you change something on "So far, I've learned…" (before your first plan): which lines you marked as not right, and their words. We stop using the answer behind each one.
  • If you turn on location (we ask only when you start a day out): your phone uses it, on the phone, to show how far your next stop is and to notice when you've arrived and left, so it can ask how the stop felt. Your location is not sent to us, with one exception: if you choose "Start from where I am" when planning a day, that start point is sent once, rounded to about 100 metres, to plan the day, and it isn't kept. You can turn location off in Your data, or in your phone's Settings.
  • If you rate a stop with no signal, the rating waits on your phone and is sent when you're back online.
  • If you turn on rating reminders (after making a plan): your phone sets them itself, for after each stop. Nothing about them is sent to us except that you turned them on or off.
  • If you speak an answer (“Tell it instead”): your phone turns your speech into text on the phone itself. The sound is never recorded, saved or sent, to us or to anyone else; only the text is sent, as if you had typed it, with a note that it was spoken.
  • If you share a moment: that you opened the share screen and whether you sent it (the kind of app it went to, never who to), and how many times your link's page was opened. Your link carries a random code, not your name.
  • If you joined through someone's share link: that link's code, so we can count how many people a share brings in. Nobody is rewarded for it.
  • If you typed a tester code on the first screen: the code. It names a group or a place where you heard about the test, never a person.
  • The three survey questions after your answers, and the two plans for a Saturday: which you'd rather do, how well each fits you (1–5), why (if you say), and the three questions before them (recharge or explore, who might come, anything you'd want in it).
  • If you plan a trip: its dates, how long, how many of you, and how you want it to feel. The trip is kept on your phone; the days we plan for it are stored like any other plan, and the trip's feelings shape only that trip.
  • If you search flights or places to stay: the search itself (where you fly from, the dates and how many of you) goes to our search providers to find results (below). We keep only which filters you used and how many results came back, never where from or when. Hotel searches use a fixed UK market, so we never need your nationality.
  • If you open a booking site from a result: that you did, which site, and whether you tell us you booked or saved it. Never what you paid or your booking details. You book and pay on that site, under its own terms. We never take payments, and we earn nothing from these links.

We don't ask for your name, email or phone number (except, if you choose, in Help & safety so a person can reply). The app knows your phone only by a random code, kept in the phone's secure storage. We don't ask for your nationality, health details or allergies. Please don't enter them.

Share-link pages

The page a share link opens counts that it was opened, and which moment it shows. It stores nothing about the person reading it: no address, no device details, and nothing stored on their device (no cookies).

Why, and on what basis

To plan days out that fit you, and to test whether this works. The lawful basis is your consent (UK GDPR Article 6(1)(a)). You can withdraw it at any time by deleting your data in the app.

Who else handles it

  • Render hosts our server and database in Frankfurt, in the EU.
  • Apple delivers the app through TestFlight, under Apple's own terms and privacy policy.
  • Duffel (flights) and LiteAPI by Nuitée (places to stay) receive a search only when you search, and only the search: route or city, dates and number of travellers, never your answers or anything that identifies you. While we test, results may be samples we make up, and the app says so.
  • Booking sites you open (Skyscanner, Google Flights, Booking.com, Airbnb, a hotel's own site) get the search in the link, and nothing else from us. What you do there is under their privacy policies.
  • No AI service receives your answers in this version of the app.

How long we keep it

Until 31 March 2027, then it's deleted. Anonymised results may be kept. Settings → Delete my data in the app removes everything we hold about your phone, straight away. If you're offline at the time, everything on your phone goes at once, and the rest is deleted from our server the next time the app is online.

Your rights

You can access, correct or delete your data, withdraw consent, or object, by using the app or by emailing abram@abdurakhmonbek.com. We reply within one month. If you're unhappy, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk).

Safety

In an emergency, call 999. For urgent medical help that isn't an emergency, call NHS 111 (you can ask for an interpreter). For anything else that feels unsafe on a day out, use Help & safety in the app: a person (Abram, or whoever is covering for him) replies within a few hours, in the app, and by phone or email if you leave one.
