You're helping design ODYS, a mobile app (iPhone and Android) that plans a day out in London around how a person wants to feel (restored, excited, connected, curious, peaceful), not around famous sights. The unit is a "moment": a specific experience at a place and time, often including how you arrive (for example, coming out of a dark Tube station into the open in front of a museum). People answer a few questions, get a day plan, follow it on the day with a quiet "Now" screen, and rate each stop afterwards. If a PDF of the screens is attached, refer to screens by their numbers.

The current look: dark all the time, one amber light, an arch symbol, a serif font (Newsreader), quiet motion. The founder feels the app isn't natural enough. He wants it to feel like nature: easy, calming and alive, so people enjoy using it.

Please research and answer:

1. What does the evidence say about nature-inspired (biophilic) design in digital interfaces? Separate what has been measured (studies, with authors, year and a link) from practice and opinion. Mark anything you can't source as UNVERIFIED.
2. Which principles carry over to a phone screen, and how? Cover light and colour, time of day and season, shape and texture, motion and easing, sound and haptics, pacing, and "soft fascination" from attention restoration theory.
3. Ten concrete patterns for this app. For each: what the person sees, why it should help, the risk (kitsch, distraction, battery, accessibility, manipulation), and how to test it with 5–10 people.
4. Real apps or products that do this well, and exactly what they do. Link each.
5. What to avoid: when "natural" design turns into decoration, slows people down, or hurts accessibility (contrast, Reduce Motion, colour blindness).
6. Light that follows the real sky: how would you make the app's colours follow the time of day and the weather while keeping text readable at every moment? Give a palette approach, not just adjectives.
7. If you could change only three things to make the biggest difference to how natural it feels, what would they be, and why?

Answer in plain English, in short sections, with sources linked. Please don't invent studies or statistics.
